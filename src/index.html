Building Enterprise AI Agent Platforms Has Become the Industry's Obsession

By Sarah Chen · March 21, 2026 · 6 min read

Building enterprise AI agent platforms has become the du jour obsession of the AI space in recent months. Every major cloud provider, from Microsoft to Google to AWS, is racing to build the definitive platform for enterprises to deploy autonomous agents.

OpenAI launched Frontier, its open platform for enterprises to build and manage AI agents, in February. In December, global research firm Gartner released a report about how governance platforms for AI agents would be the crucial infrastructure needed for enterprises to adopt the tech.

"OpenClaw gave us, gave the industry exactly what it needed at exactly the time it needed it."

The market for AI agent platforms is expected to reach $47 billion by 2028, according to recent estimates. This growth is driven by enterprises seeking to automate complex workflows that previously required human decision-making.

Nvidia clearly got the message. The chipmaker announced its own agent development kit at GTC last week, positioning itself not just as a hardware provider but as a full-stack AI platform company.

The implications for the enterprise software market are profound. Traditional SaaS vendors are scrambling to add agent capabilities to their existing platforms, while startups are building agent-first architectures from the ground up.

Security remains the biggest concern. When autonomous agents can access company data, make API calls, and trigger workflows, the attack surface expands dramatically. Several startups are already building "agent firewalls" — middleware that monitors and controls agent behavior in real-time.

The race is on, and the next 18 months will determine which platforms become the foundational infrastructure for the agent era.
